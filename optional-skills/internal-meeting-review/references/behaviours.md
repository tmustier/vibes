# The eight behaviours

These are from Roger Schwarz, [Eight Behaviors for Smarter Teams](https://schwarzassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Eight-Behaviors-for-Smarter-Teams.pdf) (13 pages, free). This file is a working summary; send people to the article for the reasoning.

The question count and the practice steps (record, reflect, revise, role-play) are from Douglas Squirrel and Jeffrey Fredrick, [Agile Conversations](https://agileconversations.com/agile-conversation-book/) (2020).

## The mindset comes first

Schwarz is clear that the behaviours are only technique without the assumptions under them:

- I have some information, and so do other people.
- Each of us may see things the others do not.
- People can disagree with me and have good motives.
- I may be contributing to the problem.

If a revised line is only a cleverer way to win the point, say so.

## What to look for

This table is for **Your part**: the lines of the person asking.

| # | Behaviour | Signs in their lines | A line to try |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State views and ask genuine questions | A view with no question after it. A question that hides a view ("Do you really think that will work?"). Questions only, with no view stated. | "I think X, because Y. What do you see differently?" |
| 2 | Share all relevant information | A fact that counts against their own preference is left out. A concern is raised after the meeting and not in it. Often only visible by asking them. | "One thing that counts against my own suggestion is…" |
| 3 | Use specific examples and agree on what important words mean | "Some of us", "always", "people feel". A loaded word such as ownership, transparency or done, used by two people in different senses. | "By ownership I mean… Is that how you are using it?" |
| 4 | Explain reasoning and intent | A request, decision or question with no reason given. | "I'm asking because…" |
| 5 | Focus on interests, not positions | The same solution repeated. Another person's solution rejected without asking what it is for. | "What is it about that option that matters to you?" |
| 6 | Test assumptions and inferences | What someone else thinks, wants or will do, stated as fact. "Obviously", "clearly". | "I'm assuming… Have I got that right?" |
| 7 | Jointly design next steps | Moving the topic on, closing a discussion or setting the next step alone. Calling someone off topic without asking how their point connects. | "I suggest we park this and decide on Thursday. Any concerns with that?" |
| 8 | Discuss undiscussable issues | Hinting. Raising it as a joke. Something they told a colleague before or after the meeting but did not say in the room, which you learn only by asking. | "I want to raise something that may be awkward, and hear how others see it." |

Notes:

- Behaviour 1 underlies the rest. If time is short, review only that one.
- Behaviour 7 is not consensus. A team can use any decision rule, as long as people know which one applies.
- Behaviour 8 starts one-to-one if that is safer, but the issue is resolved in the team.
- Not from Schwarz, but visible in a transcript: starting to speak before the other person has finished. A line that breaks off is a sign, though transcription also cuts lines, so check the context.
